# GameGameGame Invariants

This document records minimal functional requirements that should influence tests. Keep this list small and stable.

## Entity And Space

- Every meaningful game object is an entity with a stable ID.
- Entity locations are represented by occupancy of nodes in planes.
- At most one entity may occupy a node at a time.
- Plain adjacency means eight-way cardinal or intercardinal adjacency unless a contract explicitly says cardinal-only. Intercardinal adjacency is blocked when both orthogonal corner spaces between the spaces are occupied; shared Core adjacency evaluation owns this rule for adjacency-based mechanics. Explicit source-cell-link seams participate in the same intercardinal movement rule: when a diagonal move can be composed from one cardinal step plus one cardinal source-cell-link seam step, that diagonal move is legal unless both orthogonal intermediate spaces are occupied.
- Authored topology policy may add directed inventory-boundary adjacency. `ConnectsOutward` connects matching inventory boundary cells to exterior cells adjacent to the inventory owner, `ConnectsInward` connects exterior cells adjacent to the owner into preferred inventory boundary cells, and `ConnectsInwardAndOutward` combines both directions. Merged inventory aligned joins may add explicit source-cell links between owner inventory cells; each resolved `(source cell, direction)` must have zero or one destination. Merged-layer layout origins are projection metadata and must not create cross-contributor movement, POV, pathing, or adjacency by coordinate contact or overlap. Directed topology adjacency is authoritative for movement, Action Choice movement destinations, controlled-actor movement affordances, and adjacency-based interactions such as pickup.
- Traversals through containment or inventory relationships must be cycle-safe.
- Point-of-view queries for an arbitrary observer entity must reuse cycle-safe containment breadcrumbs, report the selected current-place basis, and preserve breadcrumb incomplete/cycle diagnostics instead of frontend-side ancestry guesses.
- Point-of-view target adjectives are observer-relative: one-way adjectives are derived from target-capability Action Steps in the observer's current action-plan descriptor and are applied to other entities in the observer's current place only when the matching non-mutating target-capability affordance query succeeds. Reciprocal adjectives are derived from other current-place entities' action-plan descriptors and are applied to those entities only when their matching non-mutating target-capability affordance query succeeds against the observer.

## Inventory, Bulk, And Aperture

- An entity has no usable inventory space if its inventory width or height is `0`.
- If a constrained inventory action moves entity A into or out of entity B's inventory plane, A's `Bulk` must be less than or equal to B's `Aperture`. For Pickup/Drop portability, the actor's `Bulk` must also be greater than the picked/dropped target's `Aperture`, so an object enterable by that actor is not also portable by that actor.
- Point-of-view ratio facts expose raw observer bulk and current-place aperture as `BulkToApertureRatio = observerBulk / placeAperture` before any frontend or action-step size-label thresholds are applied.
- Aperture-based action success criteria expose structured satisfaction facts as `SuccessRatio = available aperture / required bulk` where `>= 1.0` means the criterion is satisfied and `< 1.0` means it is not; logs and point-of-view adjectives may consume these ratios while frontend wording remains presentation-owned.
- Nested Enter/Exit transitions intentionally cross inventory owner apertures on both sides of the move: entering a destination inventory checks the destination owner aperture, and exiting from an inventory checks the source/container owner aperture before placing adjacent to it.
- Pickup, Drop, Transfer/Give/Take, Enter, and Exit enforce constrained Bulk/Aperture inventory transitions; Pickup and Drop also enforce the actor-bulk-greater-than-target-aperture portability gate; Teleport is an unconstrained relocation primitive and does not enforce aperture.
- Constrained inventory-boundary transformations respect nullable entity inventory policies except that the current actor's own `EnterPolicy` and `ExitPolicy` never constrain that actor's current action. Missing `EnterPolicy` defaults to first unoccupied row-major placement; missing `ExitPolicy` defaults to allowing exit from any source inventory coordinate. `FarthestFromOccupied` placement breaks ties row-major, and `EdgeAlignedWithExitDirection` exit requires the source inventory coordinate to match the selected exit edge/corner.
- Peer inventory transfer must be atomic and must respect inventory space, Bulk/Aperture transition rules, and the policy side selected by direction. Canonical `Transfer.ActorToTarget` checks the adjacent destination holder's effective `EnterPolicy` and ignores actor `ExitPolicy`; `Transfer.TargetToActor` checks the adjacent source holder's effective `ExitPolicy` and ignores actor `EnterPolicy`. Canonical `Transfer` may resolve its adjacent counterparty by authored direction or by an optional counterparty target reference. Legacy `GiveTarget`/`TakeTarget` keep deterministic first-item row-major compatibility behavior.

## Actions And Turns

- An entity is an actor only if it has a decidable Action Plan/fallback chain or decision trigger.
- Actions must produce structured traces for failed checks and resolutions.
- Action Plan resolution must distinguish failure that follows an explicit fallback from terminal resolution that ends the current root actor's turn.
- Spatial recursion may exist, but temporal recursion must be explicitly guarded.
- Canonical behavior chains execute ordered Action Steps in one Action Plan without requiring linked fallback plans.
- Canonical behavior chains continue after a failed/non-consuming step and stop after the first successful turn-consuming step.
- Runtime Action Plan overrides compose as one-turn pre/main/post slots around the default main plan; pre runs before main, main override replaces the default main plan, post runs after main fallthrough, occupied slots replace rather than stack, and slots present at turn start clear after that entity's turn resolution.
- `ApplyPrePlan`, `ApplyMainPlan`, and `ApplyPostPlan` are engine-defined canonical Action Steps that read a target reference (prefer stable target label, compatibility numeric slot) and a referenced Action Plan ID, install that referenced plan as the target entity's corresponding one-turn override slot, consume the actor's turn on success, and remain simple fallback steps rather than scripting hooks.
- Canonical behavior-chain traces must report attempted steps, state reads/writes, fallback continuation/stopping, and terminal turn outcome.
- Shared actor plan resolution must preserve canonical trace wrapping, consuming-step stop behavior, terminal non-consuming stop behavior, and all-continue terminal failure behavior across turn service, headless run, and recording consumers.
- Structured action-step attempt projections derive ordered attempts from canonical behavior-chain traces while preserving full step traces for debug expansion.
- Canonical Action Steps must preserve their documented state contracts for `Facing`, `Target`, movement, target selection, inventory transfer, fallthrough, and deterministic tie-breaks. For promoted canonical movement, successful adjacent movement sets `Facing` to the actual absolute direction moved; failed movement preserves `Facing`; and movement does not opportunistically write `Target`, because target slots/labels are refreshed before action-plan resolution from targeting rules. Canonical Push is a forced target movement primitive: a pusher may move an adjacent target one step in a selected target-relative direction when the target bulk is less than or equal to the pusher aperture and the target destination is legal/open; successful Push moves only the target and not the pusher.
- Target-path movement distance-band behavior must use legal movement topology while treating diagonal movement as more expensive than cardinal movement for authored integer `desiredDistance` bands, so maintained/orbit rings are octagonal rather than square. Successful target-path movement preserves `Target` and sets `Facing` to the actual absolute direction moved; failed target-path movement preserves `Facing`.
- Controlled actor commands for direct player/frontend input resolve through a shared Core service for move, wait, pickup, drop, enter, exit, transfer, and push; successful commands consume/advance the turn, while failed commands record a trace and do not advance the turn.
- Controlled actor affordance queries for direct player/frontend input expose Core-derived move, pickup, drop, enter, exit, transfer, and push choices with success/failure hints, but final command execution remains authoritative.
- Runtime control source is mutable Core actor state: actors marked `PlayerChoice` produce Core-owned Action Choice requests instead of being resolved by fallback policy, materialized placed-entity instances authored with `controller: Player` and legacy scenario `PlayerControls` initialize controlled entities to `PlayerChoice`, and control-source state is cloned/restored with world history. Initiative-aware stepping can advance automatic actors in deterministic actor order and pause when the next actor is `PlayerChoice`, reporting a diagnostic if no Action Choice request can be built. SadConsole consumes this stepping slice for active controlled-actor prompts, and headless scenario runs report pending prompts as runtime observations rather than auto-resolving `PlayerChoice` actors. Action Choice requests expose promoted canonical Move direction choices, first-slice Pickup/Drop source and destination choices derived from Core affordance facts, typed Enter target choices, typed Exit direction choices, Transfer counterparty/item choices with direction derived from selected item ownership, and a narrow Core-owned submission fallback for currently non-parameterized authored Action Steps while richer typed choices are promoted.
- Structured action outcome projections derive frontend log rows from structured command/action fields, expose available action success/failure criterion ratios such as aperture satisfaction, and preserve full traces for debug expansion. Core-owned action-log queries over projected outcomes must preserve structured semantics while filtering by entity/plane anchors, success/failure state, ordering chronological or newest-first, clipping after filtering/ordering, and de-duplicating combined anchor queries.
- Entity panel projections combine inspection, containment path, point-of-view current-place facts, point-of-view target adjectives, point-of-view diagnostics, action state, inventory grid, local turn-order contents, and structured local log snippets into frontend-neutral panel facts while leaving layout/focus/rendering/frontend wording frontend-owned. Actor-POV play projections compose those panel/POV facts into frontend-neutral controlled-actor, current-place, parent-chain, world-inspection-candidate, actor-inventory, and carried-inspection-candidate facts without owning layout, panning, focus, or connector geometry. Topology visibility projections expose Octagonal-distance-limited graph-reachable cells, visible entity IDs, and a wider Octagonal-distance context cell set for dimmed presentation while preserving topology/source identity and diagnostic honesty that line-of-sight/audibility are not implemented. Octagonal distance treats the observer origin as distance `0`, legal one-step topology adjacency as distance `1`, and further bands as cardinal `+1` / intercardinal `+2` expansion through directed, unblocked topology edges.
- Simulation history snapshots preserve restorable world state, coordinate lookup integrity, action state, active controlled actor, and visible trace/turn-report context so rollback cannot show future log facts on a restored frame; successful controlled-command submissions through history preserve controlled-command semantics while adding a frame interval, next snapshot, and basic actor logs from the resulting turn report, while failed controlled commands are logged on the current frame without advancing frame or world turn. The current controlled actor can be retargeted without restarting history, preserving existing intervals and rollback state. History-backed log projection derives chronological structured outcomes from successful intervals, non-controlled interval actor logs, and frame-local failed command entries, and previous-frame rollback is exposed through Core-owned history policy.

## Action Plan Data

- Entity action state such as `Facing`, numeric `Target` slots, and labeled targets is typed and persists on the actor entity across plan executions.
- Legacy action plan variables are typed, persist in invocation context, and can be written by checks before later reads while compatibility support remains.
- Canonical behavior descriptors and legacy action-plan descriptors preserve structured built-in inputs and materialize executable plans.
- The Action Step/primitive catalog describes every exposed primitive, value kind, implied state contract, and field contract.
- Action Step descriptors may expose non-mutating target-capability affordances; targeting rules can combine target-slot labels (verb), optional target templates (noun), one or more supported target capabilities (adjectives), shared actor-level Octagonal-distance targeting range, and Core-owned locality origins such as current place, own inventory, or peer inventories without executing action plans during target refresh. Shared targeting candidate preview is read-only, reports Octagonal distances, and must not mutate actor target slots. Target-consuming Action Steps may explicitly target `Self`; self targeting reads the actor ID without writing or requiring a target slot/label.
- An Action Plan descriptor has exactly one active authored shape for canonical behavior, transitional primitive, legacy low-level steps, or empty/passive state; mixed authored shapes are invalid.
- Empty canonical behavior chains are invalid for authored content and resolve as no turn if encountered at runtime.

- Root-only scenario materialization remains compatibility behavior while supported and must be distinguishable from persisted scenario/player materialization.

## Scenario Tooling

- Scenario runs use shared Content/Core services and schedule contained actors deterministically for scenario-root inventory spaces.
- Scenario reports treat expected in-simulation inability to act as runtime observation, not as an engine/runtime failure.
- Scenario run reports expose setup, actor order, history-interval-derived turn traces, final state, cycle-safe inventory/containment summaries, validation diagnostics, runtime observations, runtime failures, and capability gaps; headless runs record automatic actors through history intervals and report pending `PlayerChoice` prompts as runtime observations instead of submitting implicit player choices. Combined persisted-scenario review reports compose validation, action-plan previews, materialization, and scenario run reports without changing simulation semantics.
- Persisted scenario player narrative log reports expose a compact `player narrative projection` from shared scenario history/action-step outcome projection, default the observer to the scenario player entity, prioritize stable message IDs/args over final wording, and must not derive rows by parsing formatted trace lines; until line-of-sight/audibility exists they must not claim exact player perception. The reusable Content player-narrative projection helper owns history-to-message-ID row projection behind agent/headless reports so future frontends can consume the same structured rows without tool-specific DTO assembly.
- Scenario recording is legacy PNG/GIF fallback tooling: it captures the initial state plus one frame after each full simulated turn, writes PNG frames and a GIF for valid recordings, and produces diagnostics without artifacts for invalid authoring requests. Future visual recording should prefer history playback / SadConsole-rendered export over extending this legacy renderer.
